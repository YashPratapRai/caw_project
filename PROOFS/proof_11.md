Title: Rate Limit Bucket Eviction — Preventing OOM Under Bot Traffic
Demonstrated: The redirect rate limiter hard-caps its in-memory Map at REDIRECT_RATE_LIMIT_MAX_KEYS (default 10,000). Beyond that limit, the least-recently-seen entries are evicted.
Technical detail: On every check() call, pruneExpiredBuckets() deletes entries whose resetAt has passed. enforceBucketCap() then sorts remaining entries by lastSeenAt ascending and deletes the oldest until Map.size <= maxEntries. Cost: O(n log n) sort runs only when the cap is exceeded, not on every request.
Proof: REDIRECT_RATE_LIMIT_MAX_KEYS=100, 200 unique IPs hit GET /r/:code → Map.size stays at 100. The 100 oldest IPs lose their rate-limit state and get fresh windows on next request. Active IPs retain their counters.
Reasoning: Without a cap, a bot rotating through 500K unique IPs allocates 500K Map entries (~80 bytes each = 40MB), growing until Node.js hits its heap limit and the process is OOM-killed by the OS. The cap + eviction pattern replicates Redis's allkeys-lru maxmemory policy in-process. Tradeoff: evicted IPs can briefly exceed their rate limit on return, but this is acceptable because they were the least active clients.
